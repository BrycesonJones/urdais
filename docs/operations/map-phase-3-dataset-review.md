## Classification

**A — ready to persist and publish: 29.**
11 data center, 10 GPU compute cluster, 7 semiconductor fab, 1 power
infrastructure. By country: United States 20, Germany 3, Finland 2, Taiwan 2,
Australia 1, South Korea 1.

**B — ready to persist, research-only: 42.**
Every one of them is unplaceable: 41 have no coordinates at all and one
(`aws-morrow-county`) has city precision, which is not map-eligible. They hold
their evidence, their claims and their review notes, and they wait for a
position rather than being given one.

**C — requires human review before it is shown: 7.**

- `riken-fugaku` — the research flags it as CPU-based (A64FX) and a mismatch for
  the GPU Compute Cluster category. The decision is a taxonomy one: either a
  category exists for non-accelerator supercomputers or the record stays
  unpublished. Nothing should force it into `gpu_compute_cluster`.
- `tsmc-jasm-kumamoto`, `micron-hiroshima` — held by research §8.6 despite being
  placeable. A person lifts a researcher's hold, not a script.
- `lambda-dfw-04`, `openai-stargate-abilene` — placeable, but **no source cited
  on the record states its location**. Both sit at coordinates derived from a
  document cited on a *different* record (the Aligned DFW-04 campus, and the
  TCEQ Abilene permit). Either the positioning source is added to the record, or
  the record is not placed on its neighbour's evidence.
- `crane-clean-energy-center`, `kairos-hermes-2-oak-ridge` — power infrastructure
  whose offtaker this dataset does not hold. Crane's PPA matches Microsoft load
  across PJM; Hermes 2's matches Google load across Tennessee and Alabama.
  Neither names a facility here, and neither was given a fabricated target to
  point at. They publish if and when the offtaking campus is researched to a
  mappable standard.

**D — rejected, intentionally excluded: 38.**
Kept in `data/map/rejected-candidates.v1.json` with the candidate, the slice it
came from, the verbatim reason, and the reconsideration condition where the
reason states one. None is a facility row. The importer remarks on any batch
that re-adds one, so a rejection can be overturned by evidence but not by
forgetting.

### The seven co-located pairs

All expected, all kept as two records: CSC Kajaani / LUMI, Jülich / JUPITER,
Applied Digital Polaris Forge 1 / CoreWeave, IREN Childress / Horizon 1, Meta
New Albany / Prometheus, Aligned DFW-04 / Lambda DFW-04, Crusoe Abilene /
OpenAI Stargate Abilene.

**No `near_coordinates` candidates.** No two distinct facilities in the dataset
fall within two kilometres without being exactly co-located, so there is no
sign of a campus geocoded twice under two keys.

**No `similar_name`, `alias_collision` or `previously_rejected` candidates.** The
research's own duplicate questions — notably `meta-richland-parish` versus
`meta-hyperion`, which §8.4 says may be one Louisiana campus — arrive as
unresolved research notes instead, because their names do not resemble each
other. That pair is the most consequential open duplicate question in the
dataset and both records are research-only, so neither is on the map while it
stands.

### Edges asserted without a document

`lambda-dfw-04 → aligned-dfw-04-plano` (hosted_by), `sk-hynix-yongin` and
`sk-hynix-icheon → sk-hynix-indiana-west-lafayette` (packaging_for),
`samsung-hwaseong → samsung-pyeongtaek` (same_program). They are stored, because
the research asserts them, and they cannot gate a publication.

### Two relationships that could not be projected

`kairos-hermes-2-oak-ridge → google-tn-al-unspecified` and
`crane-clean-energy-center → microsoft-pjmunspecified`. The research deliberately
keeps these offtakers as narrative entities rather than inventing facility ids
for them, and the projection does the same: it drops the edge rather than
creating a target. This is why both power records are class C.

## Source rights

The policy, in one line: citing a fact from a public primary or government
document and linking to it is ordinary attribution, and does not pass through
the source-terms gate that governs commercial price and data feeds — that gate
exists because those feeds' values are redistributed, and a facility citation
redistributes nothing.

Every one of the 78 source domains is therefore clear by class except the six in
`data/map/source-rights-register.v1.json`, which produce the nine flags above:

- `bloomberg.com` — **review_required.** Paywalled, and the research itself
  records the claim sourced here as reported and not verified on the companies'
  own pages.
- `dispatch.com`, `dallasnews.com`, `usatoday.com`, `commercialappeal.com` —
  **verify_access.** Facts that originate behind a paywall rather than in an
  operator's own announcement. Ordinary to cite; the question is whether a
  reader following the link can check them. Two of them carry the street address
  of a published facility.
- `thefederalregister.org` — **prefer_primary.** A third-party republication of
  91 FR 34658; re-cite `federalregister.gov`.

No commercial data feed is cited anywhere in this dataset, no
`reference.source_interfaces` row was added or relaxed, and no facility evidence
claims a registered source interface. The existing terms controls are untouched.

## What Phase 4 should decide

1. **The Fugaku question.** Whether a non-accelerator supercomputer category
   exists, or whether such machines stay off the map.
2. **The two Louisiana records.** `meta-richland-parish` and `meta-hyperion` may
   be one campus under two names. Resolve before either is placed.
3. **The two positioning gaps.** Add a source that states the location to
   `lambda-dfw-04` and `openai-stargate-abilene`, or accept that they are not
   placed.
4. **The 42 unplaceable records.** They are the dataset's real growth: each needs
   a documented address or an official coordinate, not a geocoder's guess.
5. **The six rights-register entries**, and whether corroborated-only facts from
   paywalled press may carry a published record's address.
6. **Human verification.** 29 records publish on documents no person has opened.
   The evidence model already holds `human_verified` and a timestamp; nothing
   uses it yet.
