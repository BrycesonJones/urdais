## A. Executive decision record

### A.1 What UEPI V1 is

UEPI V1 is **a family of seven independent daily wholesale electricity benchmarks, one per organized U.S. power market, published in $/MWh**.

For each market, the daily value is the **arithmetic mean of that market's own authoritative hourly day-ahead benchmark price over that market's operating day**. One value per market per operating day. Nothing else.

The procedure is uniform across the seven. **The priced object is not**, and that is the central fact of this specification:

| Construct | Markets | What the number contains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Delivered price** | ERCOT, PJM, ISO-NE | The market's own published hub or RTO-aggregate day-ahead price: energy and congestion, plus marginal losses where the market prices them |
| **System energy component** | CAISO, MISO, NYISO, SPP | The uniform system marginal energy price only. Congestion and losses are excluded, because these four markets publish no footprint-wide total price and Urdais will not manufacture one by averaging hubs or zones |

Three markets publish a single series that already *is* the market's benchmark. Four do not, and each of those four publishes a system energy component that is identical at every internal location — which is the only market-wide object available without inventing an average **[verified: CAISO tariff §27.1.1.1; MISO residual identical across the eight official hubs on four tested days; NYISO residual within $0.01 across internal zones; SPP MEC within $0.0002 across 1,307 settlement locations]**.

### A.2 What UEPI V1 is not

- **Not a national composite.** There is no UEPI-US, no average of the seven, no weighted roll-up. The seven series are never summed, averaged or blended.
- **Not a price paid by load.** No series is load-weighted, and V1 does not load-weight the hours.
- **Not cross-market equivalent.** ERCOT $/MWh and CAISO $/MWh share a unit and do not share a product. A comparison chart must say so (§H).
- **Not nodal, congestion, transmission, scarcity, capacity, generation-mix, buildout, flexibility or forecast analytics.** Those live in Power Analytics and its own products, which already exist separately in Urdais.
- **Not an ISO-published index.** Every value is an Urdais calculation over public ISO data, and ERCOT's own terms disclaim the accuracy of derived compilations **[retrieved: ercot.com/help/terms §5]**.
- **Not a real-time or intraday series.** Day-ahead only, one point per operating day, no intraday tail. This matters to the frontend: UEPI uses the low-frequency range path, not the intraday one (§E).

### A.3 The decision that shapes the release

**Rights, not engineering, determine which markets ship publicly.** Under the Urdais publication policy already implemented in [`src/lib/rights/publication.ts`](../../../src/lib/rights/publication.ts):

| Publicly displayable at V1 | Ingested and stored, display blocked | Blocked outright at V1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ERCOT** (attribution), **CAISO**, **NYISO** (ambiguous → founder-accepted risk) | **PJM**, **MISO**, **SPP** (unsuitable without permission) | **ISO-NE** (no credential obtained; payload never observed) |

So the shipped public MVP is **three of seven series**. That is the honest consequence of the research, and it is not negotiable by engineering: PJM prohibits redistribution of *derived* data without membership **[retrieved]**, MISO's terms forbid derivative works **[verified]**, SPP requires written authorization from an SPP officer for commercial publication **[retrieved]**. The four remaining markets are unblocked by a signature or a credential, not by code.

The demo UEPI family in [`src/data/mock/market-detail.ts`](../../../src/data/mock/market-detail.ts) shows seven synthetic series. It is replaced by three production series. **A market without a publishable production value is removed from the selector, never left showing demo data beside production data**, and the page states which markets are withheld and why. This follows the precedent set for UGAI and UAVI, whose detail pages show an empty state rather than an invented level.

### A.4 Decisions taken in this document

1. Seven canonical benchmarks, exactly as the research recommends (§B). No alternative benchmark is substituted, and no market is "solved" where the research did not solve it.
2. One aggregation rule: arithmetic mean of the valid hours of the operating day, with **100 % hour completeness required** — no interpolation, no partial days (§C).
3. Negative and zero prices are first-class. **Percentage change is published only when both endpoints are strictly positive; the dollar change in $/MWh is published always** (§D).
4. Range semantics reuse the existing Urdais contract in [`src/lib/market-ranges.ts`](../../../src/lib/market-ranges.ts) unchanged, on its low-frequency path, plus one new requirement: the base date of every comparison is exposed to the reader (§E).
5. Three storage layers — retrieval/raw, normalized hourly, released daily — mirroring the existing `pipeline.raw_power_records` → `pipeline.power_observations` pattern from PD-2 (§F).
6. Released values are append-only and superseded, never edited (§G).
7. Comparison inside UEPI is an **absolute $/MWh overlay**, never a rebase; cross-index rebasing is suppressed when any window base is not strictly positive (§H).
8. Series identifiers are `uepi-<market>` in every layer (§7 of the deliverable list, below as §I.1).

## C. Formal UEPI V1 methodology

*This section is written to become `docs/methodology/uepi.md` at version **1.0.0-draft**. It is drafted here; it is not yet approved, and under the platform rule enforced by a database trigger, nothing may be published under a draft version. The methodology document, once approved, is the authority; the rows in `reference.methodology_versions` only identify it.*

### C.1 Objective

UEPI measures **what wholesale electrical energy cost, per megawatt-hour, in each organized U.S. wholesale power market, on each operating day, as settled in that market's day-ahead auction.**

It exists because the Information Age compute economy is increasingly a power economy, and because the price of wholesale power is the input cost underneath data-centre economics. UEPI states that cost per market. It does not model, forecast, or nationalise it.

### C.2 Observation universe

The universe is **the seven organized U.S. wholesale markets**: ERCOT, PJM, CAISO, MISO, ISO-NE, NYISO and SPP. Vertically integrated utility territories are outside the universe, because they hold no organized wholesale auction, and a utility is never an ISO/RTO peer.

For each market, the universe of observations is **the hours of that market's own operating day, as that market published them**, for the one benchmark series named in §C.4. No other location, node, hub, zone or market run enters a UEPI value.

### C.3 The unit, stated honestly

The unit is **$/MWh**, the market's own unit, published as a level. UEPI is not a base-100 index: it has no base date, no base value, and is never expressed in points. The value is signed — wholesale power prices can be negative, and UEPI keeps them negative.

### C.4 Benchmark definitions

For each market, the canonical series is exactly the one in §B.1 and no other. Restating the two constructs, because this is the clause most likely to be misread:

**Delivered-price markets.** ERCOT `HB_HUBAVG`, PJM `total_lmp_da` at pnode 1, ISO-NE Hub location 4000. Each is the market's own published benchmark and carries congestion — and, for PJM and ISO-NE, marginal losses.

**System-energy-component markets.** CAISO `MCE`, MISO `LMP − MCC − MLC` (ex-post), NYISO `LBMP − losses + congestion`, SPP `MEC` on BAA `SPP`. Each is the uniform system marginal energy price, excludes congestion and losses, and excludes — for CAISO — the marginal greenhouse-gas component `MGHG`.

**Why the split is not an inconsistency.** The four system-energy markets publish no footprint-wide total price. The alternatives were to pick one trading hub and call it the market, or to average hubs and zones into a number the market itself does not publish. The first misstates geography — MISO's Indiana Hub was $41.93 against Minnesota's $30.26 on 23 Sep 2026, and SPP's North and South Hub daily means were $23.98 and $32.84 on the same day **[verified]**. The second invents an Urdais index and presents it as the market's price. Deferring to the component the market defines as system-wide is the only option that neither misstates geography nor fabricates an aggregate.

**Every published value carries its construct label**, and no surface may show a UEPI value without it.

### C.5 Aggregation

For operating day `d` in market `m`, let `p_1 … p_n` be the valid hourly benchmark prices of that market's operating day, in source order:

```
UEPI_m,d = (1/n) · Σ_{i=1..n} p_i
```

- **Unweighted.** Every hour counts once. V1 does not load-weight hours, and does not re-weight a price the market has already weighted (PJM's RTO aggregate and NYISO's zonal LBMPs are weighted at the hourly step by their own operators; a second weighting would be a different index).
- **Signed.** Negative and zero hours enter the mean unchanged. Nothing is floored, clipped or dropped for sign.
- **Deterministic.** The mean is computed over the stored normalized hourly observations in ascending UTC interval-start order, in exact decimal arithmetic, and the result is rounded half-up to **6 decimal places** for storage. Display rounding (2 decimals) happens at the surface and never feeds a calculation.

### C.6 Expected and allowable hour counts

`n` is not a tolerance. It is a per-market assertion:

| Market | Normal day | Spring-forward | Fall-back | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ERCOT | 24 | 23 expected | 25 expected, `DSTFlag` separates the repeat | Prevailing time; **not file-verified** — assert in code, fail loudly |
| PJM | 24 | 23 expected | 25 expected, separated by `datetime_beginning_utc` | Prevailing time; **not file-verified** |
| CAISO | 24 | 23 expected | 25 expected | Prevailing time; **not file-verified** |
| MISO | 24 | **24** | **24** | Eastern Standard Time all year **[verified: 8 Mar 2026 file still has HE 1–24]** |
| ISO-NE | 24 | UNRESOLVED | UNRESOLVED | **Not file-verified**; a DST day must be parsed before release |
| NYISO | 24 | **23** (no `02:00`) | **25** (`01:00` twice) | **[verified: 8 Mar 2026 and 2 Nov 2025 files]** |
| SPP | 24 | **23** | **25** | **[verified: same two dates, separated by `GMTIntervalEnd`]** |

**Allowable `n` is exactly the expected `n` for that market and date.** There is no partial-day publication and no completeness percentage: a day that does not present its full expected hour set is not released (§G.2). A market whose DST behaviour is unverified must treat the first transition day as a release-blocking assertion failure, surfaced to an operator, not as a silent 23- or 25-hour acceptance.

### C.7 Missing observations

- A missing hour on a non-transition day **fails the day**. The day is not released and no value is written.
- **No interpolation, ever** — not linear, not carry-forward, not neighbour fill, not a seasonal estimate. A gap in the chart is the correct rendering of a day that was not published. The chart may not be made continuous by manufacturing a price.
- A failed day is not permanent. When the source later serves the complete day, the day is released then, out of order, keyed by its operating date (§G.4).

### C.8 Duplicated timestamps

Every normalized hourly observation is keyed by its **UTC interval start**, never by the market-local label. This is what makes the fall-back hour representable at all.

- **NYISO** publishes two rows labelled `01:00` with nothing to separate them **[verified: 2 Nov 2025, $53.65 and $52.59]**. UTC instants are assigned by walking the day's rows in source order from local midnight, adding one hour per row under the market's timezone rules. **Deduplicating on the timestamp string is a defect**: it drops an hour, and averaging the pair invents a price.
- **SPP** separates them with `GMTIntervalEnd` (07:00 and 08:00 on 2 Nov 2025) **[verified]**. Deduplicate on GMT, never on `Interval`.
- **PJM** separates them with `datetime_beginning_utc`; **ERCOT** with `DSTFlag`; **CAISO** with `INTERVALSTARTTIME_GMT`.
- **MISO** has no duplicate hour in its clock, by construction.

Two rows for the same UTC instant and the same benchmark are a **duplicate**, not a DST pair, and fail the hour.

### C.9 Daylight saving

The operating day is the market's own, in the market's own timezone:

- Prevailing-time markets (ERCOT, PJM, CAISO, ISO-NE, NYISO, SPP) have 23-, 24- and 25-hour operating days, and UEPI means whatever hours the market posted. A 23-hour day is a complete day, not a gap; a 25-hour day is a complete day, not a duplicate.
- **MISO is fixed at UTC−05:00 all year and always has 24 hours.** Its hours are never converted onto Eastern Prevailing Time, and its spring hour is never dropped — that hour is one MISO actually priced.
- Because market operating days differ in timezone and length, UEPI never joins two markets on a market-local clock. Cross-market alignment is by **operating date** only (§F.3).

### C.10 Nulls and non-numeric values

A blank, null or non-numeric price fails the hour, and therefore fails the day. A blank is never read as zero. For SPP, a `BAA` value other than `SPP` is not a null — it is a different market, and those rows are filtered out before completeness is assessed.

### C.11 Source revisions

Each market's revision mechanism is known to differ, and in three cases is unknown (§B.2). The methodology's position is uniform:

1. **Re-read the trailing window.** Every scheduled run re-reads the **trailing 7 operating days** for every market and recomputes them.
2. **Use the source's own currency marker where one exists.** PJM: `row_is_current = true`, and averaging without that filter double-counts corrected hours. SPP: the `RePrice` folder is checked alongside the By_Day file.
3. **A changed source value produces a new released row, never an edit.** The prior row is superseded with a reason, and the history of both survives (§G.5).
4. Revisions arriving after the trailing window — MISO's settlement calendar runs to 105 days, with disputes to 120 — are **out of scope for V1 automation** and recorded as an open item (§K). The MISO series is internal-only at V1, which bounds the consequence.

### C.12 Late-arriving observations

A day may be released late. Release order is not value order: the series is keyed by operating date, and a day released four days late takes its place in date order. The release timestamp is stored separately from the operating date precisely so that "when Urdais knew it" and "what day it describes" are never conflated.

### C.13 Backfill

Backfill is **permitted** where the source serves real timestamped history through its documented interface, and each backfilled operating day carries its own retrieval lineage, exactly as a live day does. Backfill is **not permitted** to synthesise, model, smooth or reclassify anything.

The V1 backfill target is **the trailing 400 operating days per publishable market**, which supports the 1-year range from first print with the one-further-window bound of §E intact. Where a source cannot serve that, the series begins at the first real observation and the longer ranges become available as history accumulates. A short series is an honest one.

Known backfill constraints: CAISO's 31-day query cap and HTTP 429 behaviour; PJM's 366-day request limit and 731-day archive boundary, with historic queries confined to one UTC calendar year; ERCOT's 30 requests per minute; NYISO's older days living in monthly zips rather than at the daily URL.

### C.14 No composite, no derived cross-market statistic

V1 publishes seven levels. It does not publish an average of them, a spread between them, a ratio, a national index or a "U.S. wholesale power" figure. Two markets may be drawn on one chart (§H); they are never combined into one number.

### C.15 Percentage change and dollar change

Governed by §D, which is part of this methodology and not a UI convention.

### C.16 Versioning

The methodology carries a semantic version. Approved versions are never edited in place: a change supersedes and a successor is added. A value's methodology version is frozen onto it at release. A methodology-version break between two observations makes the change across that break `null` rather than a number computed across two definitions.

## D. Negative-price and return semantics

### D.1 The problem, in the market's own numbers

On 12 April 2026, the SPP North Hub day-ahead daily mean LMP was **−$0.1083/MWh** and the South Hub's was **−$8.6282/MWh** **[verified, SPP file]**. Hourly SPP `MEC` was negative in 10 of 24 hours that day, minimum −$15.26, while the daily MEC mean stayed at +$2.7785. Negative wholesale prices are not a pathology to be filtered; they are what the market cleared at.

Ordinary percentage return breaks in several distinct ways across these values:

- a **zero denominator** is undefined;
- a **negative denominator** inverts the sign, so a price improving from −$10 to −$5 reports **−50 %** while the number rose;
- a **zero crossing** produces a mathematically defined figure that describes no rate of return anyone can act on (+$10 → −$5 reads −150 %);
- and every one of these renders in the same red-or-green percentage chip as a normal move, which is how a mathematically valid number becomes an economically deceptive one.

### D.2 The rule

**Two quantities, one always defined, one conditional.**

For an older observation `P_old` and a newer observation `P_new`, both real released UEPI values:

```
absolute_change = P_new − P_old                       always defined, unit $/MWh
percent_change  = ((P_new − P_old) / P_old) · 100     defined only when P_old > 0 AND P_new > 0
```

1. **The dollar change is always computed and always shown.** It is well defined for every sign combination, it is the quantity a power buyer actually experiences, and it is the only change figure that is never misleading.
2. **The percentage change is computed only when both endpoints are strictly positive.** Otherwise it is `null`, with a reason code — never `0 %`, never `—%` styled as a percentage, never a percentage computed from a magnitude.
3. **Direction and colour come from the sign of the dollar change**, in every case. They are never derived from the percentage, so a suppressed percentage never suppresses direction.
4. **The unit is always on the number.** `+$1.24/MWh` and `+3.4 %` are visually distinct and never interchangeable. A surface may not show a bare number that could be read as either.
5. **The same rule applies to every horizon** — today, 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year — with no exception and no per-horizon variation.
6. `null` also results, for both quantities, when there is no base observation in reach (§E) or when a methodology-version break falls between the endpoints.

### D.3 Why this rule rather than the alternatives

| Alternative | Why rejected |
| --- | --- |
| Percent whenever mathematically defined (`P_old ≠ 0`) | Produces sign-inverted returns on negative bases and nonsense magnitudes across zero. This is the deceptive-but-valid case the product must avoid. |
| Percent on absolute value of the base | Silently changes the meaning of the denominator, and still inverts direction relative to a reader's intuition on negative days. |
| Log returns | Undefined for non-positive values, and unreadable for a general audience on a price product. |
| Symmetric/arctan-style bounded change | Deterministic, but it is an invented statistic that no power-market participant quotes; the dollar change is both honest and standard. |
| Rebase every series to 100 and report index change | Moves the same zero/negative-base problem into the base period and hides it behind a synthetic level (§H). |

The adopted rule is the conservative one proposed in the brief, kept exactly, with two additions: **direction is always defined** (from the dollar change), and **reason codes are carried** so the UI and the API can explain a suppressed percentage rather than merely omitting it.

### D.4 Truth table

`Δ = P_new − P_old`. "Shown" is what the surface renders; `pct` is what the API returns for the percentage field.

| # | `P_old` | `P_new` | Example | `pct` | Reason code | Shown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | > 0 | > 0 | 30 → 33 | +10.00 % | `both_endpoints_positive` | `+$3.00/MWh` and `+10.00 %` |
| 2 | > 0 | > 0 | 30 → 27 | −10.00 % | `both_endpoints_positive` | `−$3.00/MWh` and `−10.00 %` |
| 3 | > 0 | = 0 | 30 → 0 | `null` | `new_not_positive` | `−$30.00/MWh` only |
| 4 | > 0 | < 0 | 30 → −5 | `null` | `new_not_positive` (zero crossing) | `−$35.00/MWh` only |
| 5 | = 0 | > 0 | 0 → 12 | `null` | `base_zero` | `+$12.00/MWh` only |
| 6 | = 0 | = 0 | 0 → 0 | `null` | `base_zero` | `$0.00/MWh` only |
| 7 | = 0 | < 0 | 0 → −4 | `null` | `base_zero` | `−$4.00/MWh` only |
| 8 | < 0 | < 0 | −10 → −5 | `null` | `base_negative` | `+$5.00/MWh` only (direction is **up**) |
| 9 | < 0 | < 0 | −5 → −10 | `null` | `base_negative` | `−$5.00/MWh` only (direction is **down**) |
| 10 | < 0 | = 0 | −8 → 0 | `null` | `base_negative` | `+$8.00/MWh` only |
| 11 | < 0 | > 0 | −8 → 4 | `null` | `base_negative` (zero crossing) | `+$12.00/MWh` only |
| 12 | any | any, `Δ = 0` | 30 → 30 | `0.00 %` (row 1 rule if both > 0) | `both_endpoints_positive` | `$0.00/MWh` and `0.00 %` |
| 13 | no base in reach | — | — | `null` | `no_base_observation` | change omitted entirely |
| 14 | across a methodology break | — | — | `null` | `methodology_version_break` | change omitted entirely |

Rows 8 and 9 are the reason direction must not be inferred from the percentage: both are negative-to-negative moves where the sign of `Δ` is the only correct statement of direction.

### D.5 Frontend contract consequences

The current types make movement percent-only — `MarketSnapshot.changePercent` and `PeriodPerformance.returnPercent` in [`src/types/market.ts`](../../../src/types/market.ts) — and [`src/lib/market-ranges.ts`](../../../src/lib/market-ranges.ts) guards only `first.value === 0`, so a negative base would silently produce an inverted percentage today. Both must change (§L.6). The change is additive and must not alter the rendering of any existing market: an instrument that carries no absolute-change fields keeps exactly its present behaviour.

## E. Range-selection semantics

### E.1 The point clock

Every released UEPI observation is stamped at **its operating date at 00:00:00 UTC**. The operating date is the market's own calendar date; the UTC midnight stamp is a date key, not a claim about when the hours occurred. Two consequences, both wanted:

- markets in four timezones line up on one x-axis by operating date, which is the only alignment that is meaningful across a 23-hour Central day and a 24-hour fixed-offset MISO day;
- calendar-month arithmetic on the series is exact, because it operates on UTC dates.

`asOf` for a UEPI instrument is **the timestamp of its own latest released observation**, taken from the data and never from the clock. A day published at 05:00 UTC therefore draws the same chart all day, in every browser timezone.

### E.2 The rule, reused not reinvented

UEPI adopts the existing Urdais range contract in [`src/lib/market-ranges.ts`](../../../src/lib/market-ranges.ts) — specifically its **low-frequency path** (`lowFrequencyWindowPoints`, `isLowFrequencyRangeAvailable`, `lowFrequencyPeriodReturn`), because UEPI publishes once per operating day and has no intraday tail. Copying daily points into an intraday array to unlock the 1D and 1W buttons is prohibited: it invents observations.

For range `R` and `asOf`:

| Step | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Window start** | `1D`: `asOf − 1 day`. `1W`: `asOf − 7 days`. `1M`/`3M`/`6M`/`1Y`: the same UTC calendar instant shifted back 1, 3, 6 or 12 months, keeping day and time of day. |
| **Base observation** | The **last released observation at or before the window start** — never the closest overall, never an interpolation, never the oldest available point. |
| **Base bound** | The base must itself fall no earlier than **one further window back** (`rangeStart(R, rangeStart(R, asOf))`). A `1D` base must lie within the preceding day of the window start; a `1M` base within the preceding month. |
| **Availability** | A range is offered only when a base exists within that bound **and** the window holds at least two real observations. Otherwise the button is shown disabled — never enabled over a fabricated line. |
| **Window contents** | Every released observation from the base to the latest, inclusive. |
| **Change over the range** | §D applied to (base, latest). |

This is "closest observation on or before the boundary, bounded", not a trading-day count. Urdais markets are quoted on calendar days, and a wholesale power market operates seven days a week, so a five-day trading week would be the wrong model here.

### E.3 Label honesty

The bound above exists so a label cannot drift far from what it measures, but within the bound the base may still sit a day or two off the exact boundary — that is the price of not interpolating. The product closes the gap by **stating the base**, not by silently trusting the label:

- Every change figure exposes `baseOperatingDate` and `latestOperatingDate` in the API, and the surface shows them (tooltip or caption: "since 2026-09-22").
- Where the base is not the exact boundary date, that is visible from those dates rather than hidden behind "1 month".
- A range whose base falls outside the bound is **unavailable**, not silently stretched.

### E.4 Worked cases

| Case | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Yesterday released normally | `1D` base = yesterday. Change is a one-day change. |
| Yesterday failed completeness, the day before released | `1D` base = two days back, still inside the bound. Change is shown, and the caption says "since" that date. |
| Three consecutive days failed | `1D` unavailable (no base inside the bound). `1W` still available. Nothing is invented to fill the button. |
| Series is 40 days old | `1D`, `1W`, `1M` available; `3M`, `6M`, `1Y` disabled until history reaches them. |
| A day is released late, filling a hole | It enters in operating-date order and the affected ranges recompute deterministically from the same stored points. |

No per-market tables. No adapter-specific schemas. No node dimension table (UEPI reads one locator per market; a node catalogue belongs to nodal analytics, which is out of scope). No hourly publication API. No intraday series. No composite table. No forecast, capacity, congestion or generation-mix column anywhere in this model.

## G. Release and revision policy

### G.1 When a daily value may be released

All of the following, evaluated per market per operating day:

1. **The operating day has begun** in the market's own timezone. Day-ahead prices for day `d` are published on `d−1`, so a value could technically be computed before its day starts; UEPI does not release a future-dated point. The latest point on the chart is always today's delivery day or earlier.
2. **Hour completeness is exact**: `observation_count = expected_observation_count` for that market and date (§C.6).
3. **Every hour is `accepted`** — no `suspect` hour enters a mean.
4. **Every quality check in §G.3 passes.**
5. **The methodology version in force is `approved`**, not draft.
6. **The rights gate allows the purpose.** `mayPublishSourceValue` decides display; a market whose decision is `blocked_*` still calculates and stores, and is simply not served publicly (§J).

Failing 1–5 means **no row is written**; the day is recorded as failed in the run ledger with its reason. Failing 6 means the row is written and withheld.

### G.2 No partial days

There is no completeness threshold below 100 %, and no "publish the mean of the hours that exist" path for a normal day. A 23-hour spring day is complete at 23 hours; a 23-hour day in June is a hole and fails. This is the single rule that keeps a UEPI level from quietly meaning "most of a day".

### G.3 Quality checks

Each is named, each writes its measured margin into `quality_checks`, and each is a release gate.

| Check | Markets | Assertion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hour_completeness` | all | count equals the expected count for that date |
| `no_duplicate_interval` | all | no two accepted hours share a UTC interval start |
| `contiguous_hours` | all | interval starts are consecutive hours spanning the operating day |
| `system_component_uniformity` | CAISO | `MCE` at a second and third trading hub matches within $0.0001 per hour **[verified: spread was exactly 0.000000 on 23 Sep 2026]** |
| `system_component_uniformity` | MISO | `LMP − MCC − MLC` at a second official hub matches within $0.0001 per hour **[verified: spread 0.0000 on four days]** |
| `system_component_uniformity` | NYISO | λ at a second internal zone matches within **$0.02** per hour — cent rounding, not a second price **[verified: $0.01 on 23 Sep 2026, $0.02 in March 2026]** |
| `system_component_uniformity` | SPP | `MEC` at a second BAA-`SPP` settlement location matches within $0.001 per hour **[verified: 0.0002 across 1,307 locations]** |
| `component_identity` | PJM | `system_energy_price_da + congestion_price_da + marginal_loss_price_da` reproduces `total_lmp_da` within $0.01 |
| `component_identity` | CAISO | `MCE + MCC + MCL + MGHG` reproduces `LMP` within $0.00001. **A parser that assumes a three-part LMP is wrong** |
| `component_identity` | SPP | `MEC + MCC + MLC` reproduces `LMP` within $0.001 |
| `scope_filter` | SPP | where the `BAA` column exists, every row used has `BAA = SPP`; `SWPW` rows are excluded and counted |
| `scope_filter` | NYISO | the carrier zone is one of the eleven internal zones; `H Q`, `NPX`, `O H`, `PJM` never enter |
| `scope_filter` | MISO | the carrier node is one of the eight official hubs by name; the broad `Type = Hub` flag is never used as a filter |
| `version_currency` | PJM | every row used has `row_is_current = true` |
| `published_column_used` | ERCOT | the value is the published `HB_HUBAVG` column, not a recomputed four-hub mean (the two differ by up to $0.005 **[verified]**) |
| `plausibility_guard` | all | the absolute hourly price is ≤ 25,000 $/MWh. **This is an operational guard against a parse error, not a methodological claim about offer caps**; a breach marks the hour `suspect` and fails the day for operator review |

### G.4 Late data

A failed day is retried on every scheduled run while it remains inside the trailing re-read window, and may be released whenever it becomes complete. Older gaps are filled by an operator-triggered backfill for a named date range. Both paths write `release_kind` so the ledger shows which produced a point.

### G.5 Corrections and supersession

A released value is **never updated in place** and **never deleted**. When a re-read produces a different value for a released day:

1. The affected hourly observations are superseded (new rows; old rows keep `superseded_by_id`).
2. A new `uepi_daily_values` row is inserted with `revision_of_id` pointing at the superseded release and a `supersession_reason` naming the cause (`source_revision`, `source_version_increment`, `reprice_file`, `operator_correction`).
3. The prior row is marked superseded at the same instant.
4. Public reads serve the current row; the API exposes the revision chain on request, so a reader who charted the old value can find out what happened to it.

Supersession is also the only mechanism for a methodology change: values published under an earlier approved version are not recomputed retroactively unless that version is itself superseded and the change is stated. Approved methodology versions are never edited in place.

### G.6 Schedule

One daily cron, `/api/cron/uepi`, at **10:15 UTC** — after the existing power crons, and at a wall-clock time (05:15 ET / 04:15 CT / 03:15 PT) at which all seven markets are inside operating day `d` locally, so a single run can release `d` for every market and re-read `d−1 … d−7`. The route is authenticated with `CRON_SECRET` like the other Urdais crons.

## H. Comparison-mode specification

### H.1 Inside UEPI: absolute $/MWh, never rebased

The seven UEPI series share one unit, so the comparison the UI already builds for instruments in a family — `basis: "absolute"` in [`src/data/mock/market-detail.ts`](../../../src/data/mock/market-detail.ts) — is the right one and needs no change in kind. Two markets are drawn on one $/MWh axis at their real values.

This is better than rebasing for three reasons: the axis stays in the unit the reader came for; **it is immune to the zero and negative base problem**, which rebasing is not; and the level difference between markets is itself the interesting fact (the SPP-versus-PJM gap is information, and a rebase deletes it).

**It is not a licence to imply equivalence.** ERCOT's delivered hub price and CAISO's system energy component are different economic objects that happen to share a unit. Therefore:

- Every comparison view labels **both** series with their construct: "delivered price (energy + congestion + losses)" or "system energy component (excludes congestion and losses)".
- Where the two selected series have **different constructs**, the chart shows a persistent caption saying so in one sentence. This is a product requirement, not a tooltip nicety: without it the comparison overclaims.
- No spread, basis, ratio or difference series is computed between two markets (§C.14). The eye may compare; the product does not publish a derived cross-market number.

### H.2 Across indices: rebasing, and when it is refused

The detail page also offers UEPI against other Urdais indices (UCPI, UMPI, …) with `basis: "relative"`, which rebases both series to percentage change from the first point of the window in [`src/components/charts/detailed-market-chart.tsx`](../../../src/components/charts/detailed-market-chart.tsx). That function today computes `relative && base !== 0 ? (value / base − 1) × 100 : value`, which has two defects the moment a UEPI series enters it:

- with `base = 0` it plots **raw values on an axis labelled `%`**, mixing units silently;
- with `base < 0` it plots **sign-inverted percentages**, so a rising series falls on the chart.

The rule:

> **A rebased comparison is rendered only when the window base of every plotted series is strictly positive.** Otherwise the comparison is not drawn: the primary series is shown alone, and the surface states that the comparison is unavailable for this range because a base value is not positive. Another range may be selected, and shorter or longer windows frequently have a positive base.

Rebasing to 100 rather than to percent change is the same transform with a different offset and fails identically; it is not a workaround, and it is not adopted.

### H.3 Why not normalize with a safer transform

Considered and rejected for V1: rebasing on the window mean (invents a reference nobody quotes), on the absolute value of the base (inverts direction), min-max scaling (makes two series look like they share a range when they do not), and plotting the dollar difference from base for each series (defensible, but the resulting axis is "change in each series' own unit", which is only meaningful when units match — and when units match, §H.1's absolute overlay is already available and is simpler).

The MVP position is therefore: **within UEPI, absolute overlay in $/MWh with construct labels; across indices, rebase only when every base is strictly positive, otherwise refuse and say why.**

The ids are client state only and appear in no URL; the only cross-reference to fix is the comment in [`src/types/power-analytics.ts`](../../../src/types/power-analytics.ts).
- **Power Delivery identities are untouched.** `reference.grid_areas` keeps its own slugs and uuids: a physical balancing-authority area is not a price series, and PD-2 already states that its identity is independent of UEPI.

### I.3 Known limitations, per market, published

These are not internal caveats. They are the published text, because a reader comparing two UEPI series needs them to read the chart correctly.

- **All markets.** UEPI is an Urdais calculation over public market data, not an index published by the ISO or RTO. It is a day-ahead auction benchmark, not the price paid by load, and not a real-time price.
- **ERCOT.** The day-ahead hub average is a shift-factor construct, not the simple mean of the four regional hub prices (the two differed by up to $0.005/MWh on 25 Sep 2026). The written definition changed effective 1 Sep 2019 under NPRR931; history that spans that date spans two definitions.
- **CAISO.** Energy component only: congestion, losses and the marginal greenhouse-gas component are excluded. Whether `MGHG` should be included is an open methodology question (§K).
- **MISO.** Energy component derived as `LMP − MCC − MLC` from the ex-post file; MISO publishes no MEC column. Hours are Eastern *Standard* Time all year, so a MISO day never has 23 or 25 hours.
- **NYISO.** Reference-bus energy component derived from a zonal row; NYISO publishes no statewide LBMP and no hub. Zonal prices, including New York City, can differ materially from this reference price.
- **SPP.** Energy component for the SPP balancing authority only; the western `SWPW` market is excluded. SPP's own trading hubs diverge from each other and from this series — their daily means were −$0.11 and −$8.63 on 12 April 2026.
- **PJM.** RTO-aggregate total LMP; Western Hub, the traded benchmark, is a different and narrower price.
- **ISO-NE.** Hub total LMP, defined by tariff as the arithmetic average of the Hub's nodes; the Hub node list is revisable without the location id changing, so the hub-definition vintage is pinned in metadata.
- **Negative prices.** Values can be zero or negative. Where they are, UEPI shows the change in $/MWh and shows no percentage, by design (§D).

```ts
function normalizeOperatingDay(
  benchmark: BenchmarkDefinition,
  result: AdapterResult,
): { hours: NormalizedHour[]; expectedCount: number; failures: NormalizationFailure[] };
```

Rules the normalizer owns, and no other layer may re-decide:

- **UTC assignment** per §F.3 (source UTC column; else row-order walk from local midnight; else fixed offset for MISO).
- **Expected count** from the benchmark definition and the date (§C.6), computed from the IANA zone, not from a hard-coded calendar.
- **Decimal arithmetic.** Prices and means are computed in exact decimal. A float pipeline will not reproduce `input_digest` across machines.
- **No value is ever dropped for sign**; a `suspect` status comes only from the plausibility guard or a parse failure.

### L.8 Known edge cases, enumerated for the implementer

1. NYISO's duplicated `01:00` — dedupe and you lose an hour; average the pair and you invent a price.
2. SPP's `BAA` column appearing mid-2026 — a positional parser silently reads the wrong column after the break.
3. Participant hubs whose names contain `HUB` (SPP `CSWS_HUB`, MISO's broad `Type = Hub`) are not market prices.
4. MISO's ex-ante file is a different price, by up to $6.17/hour, at a nearly identical URL.
5. MISO's EST clock joined to a prevailing-time market on a DST day is wrong by an hour in both directions.
6. CAISO's fifth component: `LMP = MCE + MCC + MCL` is now false.
7. CAISO returns HTTP 429 on repeat calls, and caps queries at 31 days.
8. PJM rows are versioned; ignoring `row_is_current` double-counts corrected hours. PJM's display name for pnode 1 differs between feeds.
9. ERCOT's ID token expires hourly, and the rate limit is 30/min; new hubs (`HB_PAN`, LRGV) appear on the same report and must never enter `HB_HUBAVG`.
10. NYISO's daily URL 404s for older days; the monthly zip is the path, and a 404 there is a real error.
11. A negative daily value must survive the whole path — calculation, storage, API, chart y-axis and change display — without being clipped, and the chart's y-domain must accommodate values below zero.
12. A 23-hour day is complete; a 25-hour day is complete; a 23-hour day in June is not.
13. Backfill ordering: releases arrive out of operating-date order and must not disturb the series key.
14. `HB_HUBAVG` spans a definition change effective 1 Sep 2019 (NPRR931) — relevant only if backfill ever reaches that far.

### L.9 Expected production verification

Per the Urdais product closeout gate, **no data product is operationally complete until an unattended scheduled rollover is observed end to end.** For UEPI that means all of:

1. The cron is registered in `vercel.json` and authenticated with `CRON_SECRET`.
2. A scheduled run, triggered by nobody, releases a new operating day for each of the three publishable markets, and the run ledger records it.
3. The following day, a second unattended run rolls the series forward again, and the trailing re-read leaves the prior days unchanged (or supersedes one, visibly and with a reason).
4. `/markets/uepi` shows the new value, the new operating date, the hour count, the methodology version and the rights notice — with no demo instrument anywhere on the page.
5. `/api/uepi` matches the page exactly, and contains no PJM, MISO, SPP or ISO-NE series.
6. A production query proves that every released row reproduces its `input_digest` from its stored hours.
7. The freshness monitor covers the seven series and fails loudly on a stale publishable market, consistent with the existing 48-hour convention.

### L.10 Out of scope, restated because it is where scope creep will try to enter

Nodal or zonal breakdowns; congestion or loss analytics; a spread between markets; real-time prices; forecasts of any kind; scarcity, capacity, buildout or flexibility metrics; generation mix; a national composite; load weighting; any intraday UEPI series; and any new abstraction layer justified by "there could be more markets later". There are seven.
